# PHASE 7 — Adversarial Review & Revision Loop

**Objective.** Break the DT-GSK manuscript with two hostile referees — R1 (Q1) and
R2 (Q2) — before a real editor does, then revise until R1 has zero open *majors*
and R2 zero open *must-fixes*, keeping a point-by-point revision log that later
seeds the rebuttal.

This file expands **Phase 7** and **Part 9** of `papers/PAPER_BUILD_PROMPT.md`. It
follows `PHASE_6_prose_quality.md` (which hands over a style-passed, fact-frozen full
draft) and hands to `PHASE_8_compliance.md` (final build + constraint proofs). It
also produces the artifact Phase 9 consumes: the completed `revision_log.md`,
which is the raw material for the reviewer-response letter (Appendix E of the
build prompt).

Scope reminder from Part 0's contract: this phase may **rewrite** prose, restructure
sections, add or move exhibits, and tighten claims — but it may **not** invent
citations outside the 57 admissible keys (Appendix A / C1), may **not** introduce
a number that is not bound to committed data + commit SHA (C2), and may **not**
change a reported result to make a reviewer happy. Every fix is either a legitimate
analysis already sitting in `results/` and `results/_analysis/`, a re-derivation
from committed CSVs via a named `papers/scripts/` generator, or an honest
narrowing of a claim.

## Tasks

### 7.1 — R1 (Q1) referee pass

Adopt R1: an adversarial Q1 reviewer holding the paper to a *Swarm and Evolutionary
Computation* / *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation* standard. R1 assumes
nothing, trusts nothing unproven, and reads the honesty clauses (C7) hardest of all.
Score each Part-9.1 rubric dimension **1–5** with **specific evidence** (a section,
table, figure, or sentence, quoted). Per the rubric rule, **every score ≤3 becomes a
revision ticket.**

**Rubric dimensions (Part 9.1), what R1 demands for a 4–5:**

- **Novelty.** SGSM (the interaction-structure memory) is delimited crisply from
  differential grouping (`omidvar2014dg`), eigenvector crossover (`guo2015eig`),
  and CMA-ES covariance adaptation (`hansen2001cmaes`, `mohamed2017lshadespacma`).
  The claim is "structure learned *for free* from the GSK dynamics," not "another
  linkage learner"; the contrast must be explicit, not implied.
- **Baselines.** The 7-algorithm GSK family (gsk `mohamed2020gaining`, agsk
  `mohamed2020agsk`, apgsk `apgsk2021`, fdb-agsk `fdbagsk2023`, atmals-gsk
  `alfadli2025atmals`, egsk `jawad2024egsk`, dt-gsk) is strong and *fairly tuned*,
  and the same-family scope is **justified as a controlled contribution study**,
  not excused as convenient.
- **Statistics.** Correct tests and corrections: Friedman ranks (`friedman1937use`,
  `demsar2006statistical`) + Nemenyi CD; pairwise Wilcoxon (`wilcoxon1945individual`)
  with Holm (`holm1979simple`) *and* Benjamini–Hochberg (`benjamini1995controlling`);
  A12 effect sizes (`vargha2000critique`); BCa CIs (`efron1993introduction`). No
  "significant" without a test; N and MaxFES stated; ties explicit.
- **Reproducibility.** Seed formula `get_cec_seed(20240620, dim, func, run) %
  2147483646 + 1`, run counts (CEC2017: 51; CEC2011: 25; CEC2013: 51), FP-regime sentinel
  (`environment.json`), byte-identity-locked ISM core, and a per-headline-number
  commit SHA are all present and checkable.
- **Ablations.** Each DT-GSK mechanism earns its place empirically — the ablation
  in `sections/performance.tex` / the supplement shows what breaks when it is removed.
- **Honesty.** Losses are reported, not buried — including the D30 result (DT-GSK
  is family #2 at D30 behind EGSK; #1 overall and at D10/D50/D100). No suspiciously
  clean sweep.
- **Threats to validity.** Benchmark overfitting (CEC2017 primary; CEC2013 second
  comparison suite; CEC2011 real-world), dimension-scope generality, and the
  SLSQP-substitutes-fmincon caveat for EGSK are named and bounded.

**Example R1 comments an DT-GSK paper would realistically draw** (write these as
first-pass entries, then answer honestly with evidence — do not pre-soften them):

1. **[R1-N] Novelty vs differential grouping unclear.** "The manuscript calls SGSM
   'interaction-structure memory,' but §3 does not separate it from differential
   grouping (`omidvar2014dg`) or eigenvector crossover (`guo2015eig`). Is SGSM a
   variable-interaction learner or a search-direction memory? State the distinction
   formally and show what SGSM captures that DG does not." *(Novelty; likely major
   until §3 adds the explicit contrast paragraph + a one-line formal delimiter.)*

2. **[R1-B] Same-family-only baselines.** "Comparing only GSK variants risks a
   closed-world result. Why no L-SHADE-cma (`mohamed2017lshadespacma`) or a

   do NOT add a new competitor run unless it already exists in `results/_run_all/` —
   if it does not, defend the scope, do not fabricate.)*

3. **[R1-D30] Low-D / D30 regression unexplained.** "DT-GSK trails EGSK at D30 while
   leading at D10/D50/D100. This inversion is asserted but not explained. Is it a
   structural consequence of subsystem gating to high-D, or noise?" *(Honesty +
   Threats; must point to the ablation/limitation subsection; the answer is the
   *structural* explanation — subsystems gated to high-D — not a tuning promise.)*

4. **[R1-A12] A12 reported but CIs missing on headline gaps.** "Vargha–Delaney A12

   gap has no interval. Add BCa CIs (`efron1993introduction`) so the effect size is
   bounded, not point-estimated." *(Statistics; fix by surfacing `tables/T16_bca.tex`
   / `generate_t16_bca.py` output into the main text, not the supplement.)*

5. **[R1-REPRO] Per-cell commit SHA missing.** "The seed formula and run counts are
   stated, but individual result cells are not traceable to a commit. A reader cannot
   audit which SHA produced Table Txx." *(Reproducibility; fix by wiring the Appendix
   D.1 ledger's `commit_sha` column into the reproducibility appendix / caption
   footnotes.)*

6. **[R1-HOLM] Two corrections without a stated primary.** "Both Holm (`holm1979simple`)
   and Benjamini–Hochberg (`benjamini1995controlling`) are reported. Which controls
   the confirmatory family, and which is exploratory? Reporting both without a
   pre-registered choice invites cherry-picking." *(Statistics; fix by declaring Holm
   as the confirmatory FWER control and BH as the FDR sensitivity check in §4.)*

7. **[R1-TUNE] Baseline tuning parity.** "Were all seven family members run under
   identical budgets (MaxFES, population schedule) and seeds? If DT-GSK enjoys a
   larger local-search budget, the comparison is confounded." *(Baselines; answer

8. **[R1-OVERFIT] CEC2017 overfitting risk.** "Design choices tuned on CEC2017 may

### 7.3 — Triage & revise

Merge both reports into a single ticket ledger and drive it to the exit condition.

**Severity labels (assign one per ticket):**

- **major** — R1-owned; a Q1 referee would reject or demand major revision over it
  (novelty gap, statistical error, unfair baseline, missing reproducibility hook,
  buried loss). **Must reach zero open.**
- **must-fix** — R2-owned; blocks acceptance at Q2 (undefined symbol, illegible
  figure, over-length section, unreferenced/uninterpreted exhibit, broken formatting).
  **Must reach zero open.**
- **minor** — real but non-blocking (a clarification, an extra sentence of context).
  Fix where cheap; may be deferred with a logged reason.
- **nice-to-have** — cosmetic or stylistic polish. Optional; never blocks the gate.

**Ticket schema** (in `papers/tickets.md` or inline in the review files):

```
id | persona (R1/R2) | dimension | severity | status (open/in-progress/resolved/deferred)
   | location(s) | required change | owner-persona | resolving commit | log-ref
```

**Revision procedure (loop until the gate holds):**

1. Sort tickets: majors and must-fixes first, then minors, then nice-to-haves.
2. For each, make the *substantive* change in the `.tex` source (or regenerate the
   exhibit via its named `papers/scripts/` generator when the fix is a figure/table).
   Never respond cosmetically to a substantive objection (see Pitfalls).
3. If a fix needs a number, that number must already exist in committed data
   (`results/_run_all/…`, `results/_analysis/…`) and enter via a named generator so
   the C2 binding survives. If the number does not exist, the honest move is to
   **narrow the claim**, not to run-and-report-inline-untracked.
4. If a fix would require a citation outside the 57, **do not add it** (C1). Rewrite
   the claim so it stands on an admissible key or on the paper's own evidence.
5. Log every change point-by-point in `papers/revision_log.md` (schema below) — this
   is the seed for the Phase 9 rebuttal (Appendix E).
6. Re-score the touched rubric dimension. A major/must-fix is only *resolved* when
   its dimension would now score ≥4 (R1) or pass (R2) on a fresh read.

**Revision-log schema** (`papers/revision_log.md`):

```
### [ticket-id] — <one-line title> (severity: <..>, persona: <R1/R2>)
- Comment (verbatim): "<the referee objection>"
- Change: <what was actually changed, in the source>
- Now addressed in: §<sec> / Table Txx / Figure yy (commit <SHA>)
- Residual/declined: <none | principled reason if partially declined>
```

Keep the log append-only within the phase; do not rewrite history — the rebuttal
needs the honest trail.

### 7.4 — Re-verify after revision

Revision can silently break C1/C2 or blow the page budget. After the loop reaches
zero open majors/must-fixes, re-run the verification pass **before** declaring the
gate met.

- **C1 — citation closure (Appendix D.2).** Parse `references.bib` → bibkeys; scan
  `papers/**/*.tex` for `\cite{…}`; assert `cites ⊆ bibkeys` **and** every one of the
  57 keys is used at least once. Output must print **zero undefined, zero uncited**.
  Confirm no ticket introduced a key outside the 57 and none dropped a key to zero
  uses.
- **C2 — number binding (Appendix D.3).** For every table cell and figure point
  touched during revision, re-assert the value equals what the named generator emits
  from the committed CSV, and that the Appendix D.1 ledger row (with `commit_sha`)
  still matches. Any exhibit regenerated in 7.3 gets a fresh ledger row.
- **No new undefined `\ref`/`\cite`.** Recompile via `build_pdf.py` /
  `build_supplementary.py` and grep the logs for "undefined" (references, citations,
  labels). Cross-check against the pre-revision baseline: the count must not increase.
- **Page budget still holds (C3 / §3.4).** Recount main-text typeset pages; confirm
  ≤ the 16–22 pp envelope, and that any content moved in a length ticket landed in
  the supplement rather than being deleted. If over, migrate to supplement before
  shrinking figures (never shrink a figure below R2 legibility to win pages).

Record the three check outputs (C1 pass, C2 pass, compile-clean + page count) at the
end of `revision_log.md`. `papers/scripts/generate_review_pack.py` may be used to
assemble the review artifacts, but the C1/C2/build checks are the authoritative
gates.

## Pitfalls & anti-patterns

- **Cosmetic responses that don't fix the substance.** Answering "novelty unclear"
  by adding the word "novel" to the abstract, or "baselines too narrow" with a
  sentence of hand-waving, leaves the major open. A major closes only when the
  underlying gap is actually closed and the dimension would re-score ≥4. Re-reading,
  not re-wording, is the test.
- **Fixing prose but breaking a number binding.** Rewriting a results sentence and
  editing a value "to match" without re-deriving it from committed data severs the
  C2 binding. Every number touched must still trace to a named generator + CSV +
  commit SHA. Re-run the D.3 check after any results-section edit.
- **Adding a citation outside the 57 to satisfy a reviewer (forbidden).** If R1 asks
  "why not compare to method X," the answer is *never* "we now cite `someNewKey2025`."
  It is either an admissible-key reframe (e.g. position against `omidvar2014dg`,
  `guo2015eig`, `mohamed2017lshadespacma`, `awad2017ensemble`, `brest2017single`
  which are already in the 57) or a principled, logged narrowing of scope. Adding a
  58th key violates C1 and fails Phase 8.
- **Inventing results to answer "add an external baseline."** Do not run an untracked
  comparator and paste numbers inline. Either the run already exists in
  `results/_run_all/` with a ledger row, or the claim is narrowed. The same-family
  scope is a *defensible design choice*, not a hole to be plugged with fabricated data.
- **Softening an honest loss to dodge a comment.** The D30 second-place result and the
  EGSK SLSQP-vs-fmincon substitution are disclosed on purpose (C7). "Fixing" a
  reviewer's discomfort by deleting or blurring these trades a minor comment for an
  integrity failure. Explain the loss; do not hide it.
- **Winning page budget by starving figures.** Shrinking a CD or convergence figure
  below R2's print-legibility bar to reclaim a §3.4 page reopens a must-fix. Migrate
  content to the supplement instead (C4 order: migrate before shrink).
- **Declaring done with open majors/must-fixes.** The gate is *zero* open majors (R1)
  and *zero* open must-fixes (R2) — not "mostly addressed." A deferred minor is fine
  with a logged reason; a deferred major is not a pass.
- **Rewriting the revision log to look cleaner.** The log is evidence for the Phase 9
  rebuttal; it must record what was actually objected to and actually changed. A
  sanitized log produces a rebuttal that doesn't match the manuscript's history.
- **Letting the Phase 6 prose-quality pass regress.** Heavy revision can reintroduce banned
  phrasing (Appendix C) or uniform rhythm. Spot-run the A1 tell-scan on any
  substantially rewritten paragraph so Phase 6's exit gate still holds.

## Exit gate

- [ ] **R1 has zero open majors.** Every score-≤3 dimension re-scored ≥4 on a fresh
      read; `review_R1.md` scorecard and ticket list show no open major.
- [ ] **R2 has zero open must-fixes.** `review_R2.md` shows every must-fix resolved;
      only nice-to-haves (optional) may remain.
- [ ] **Revision log complete.** `revision_log.md` has a point-by-point entry for
