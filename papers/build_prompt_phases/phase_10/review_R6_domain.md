## 1. Distinction-defense (SGSM vs differential grouping; linkage/eigenframe positioning) — 4/5

**What holds up.** The four-axis positioning in Related Work §2.2 (update trigger / evaluation cost / what is learned / how exploited) is a genuine, well-articulated distinction. Differential grouping is correctly characterized as an *offline* stage spending `O(n^2/m)` objective evaluations to learn a *hard partition* for CC decomposition; CMA-ES as a full sampling covariance; eigen-crossover as an *instantaneous* population statistic. ISM is correctly placed as online, accepted-move-only, zero-extra-objective-eval, learning a *soft signed pair graph* exploited *discretely* (block masks + bounded subspace + one terminal eigenbasis). The non-claims are disciplined: no true-separability recovery, no rotational invariance, `D=100` evidence ceiling, ACE "not an instance" of any bandit policy with no regret transfer. This is better than most published positioning.

**Why not 5.**
- **Loaded terminology for a co-movement statistic.** `G` is updated from accepted-move deltas (Eq. sgsm-graph) — a basis- and operator-dependent *co-movement* EMA. The paper itself concedes it does not recover the objective's interaction structure. Yet "interaction-structure memory" and "linkage-aware" borrow the authority of the variable-interaction/linkage-learning literature (it cites Omidvar DG to do so). On the rotated CEC functions, the sub-`D50` "linkage" blocks are *contiguous coordinate groups* over an arbitrary index order — meaningless with respect to any true variable grouping. A linkage/LSGO reviewer will read the naming as overreach. The caveats exist but are diffuse; a single explicit "these are co-movement statistics over coordinate indices, not recovered objective linkage" at first use would close it.
- **The distinction is never tested.** The whole thesis is GSK's "structure blindness," and the paper positions ISM against DG/CMA-ES/eigen-crossover — but the empirical panel contains *zero* structure-learning optimizers (see §4 below). The distinction is therefore argued but not demonstrated.

Ticket: T-07 (terminology, editorial).

## 2. Overhead-honesty ("no additional objective evaluations"; O(D^2)/O(D^3)) — 5/5

This is the strongest part of the paper, with explicit supporting evidence, so I award 5.
- `complexity_analysis.md` has an explicit "Wording guards (QA)": report SGSM/polish as `O(D^2)`/`O(D^3)` compute "**never as 'free'**," and "Do not claim overall complexity better than GSK."
- The `fig:taxonomy` caption literally states: *not "free": bookkeeping cost is `O(NP·D)` per generation*.
- The method scopes the zero-cost claim precisely to graph *learning* ("it observes moves the run already evaluated") and explicitly charges the two *exploitation* channels — the subspace local search and every eigenframe compass probe — through the `BudgetController` ("RNG-free but not evaluation-free"). The `O(D^3)` eigendecomposition is stated as once-per-run; the `O(D^2)` refresh amortized over the 5-generation cadence.
- The claim is backed by *measured* cost: Table `tab:runtime` shows DT-GSK is the **most expensive panel member at D10/30/50** (6.92 vs GSK 0.55 s at D10 — ~12×), with no runtime-superiority claim made in either direction, and the tuning disclosure even concedes the polish *costs* budget on smooth functions (F3/F12 at D100). This is honest to a fault.

No ticket required. (One forward-looking note, not a defect: the memory footprint claim "80 KB at D=100" is trivially true; fine.)

## 3. Leakage-risk — 3/5 (tickets required)

**T-02 (major, editorial) — CEC2013's evidentiary status is contradictory across documents and breaks frozen wording.** The main paper (performance.tex L522–524) states CEC2013 "is reported as a second comparison suite … and carries **no independence claim of any kind**." Governance PR-03/RS-11 make this a hard rule: *"Calling CEC2013 'independent', 'holdout', or 'validation' in the main text"* is prohibited, and "'hold-out' survives ONLY as the frozen name of the Phase-12 SGSM-overlay ablation design." **The supplement violates this for the general vs-GSK CEC2013 tables (T11–T14):** the subsection is titled *"CEC 2013: External Hold-Out,"* opens *"As an external hold-out validation,"* asserts the ranking *"transfers to a structurally distinct benchmark suite that was not consulted during tuning,"* and stamps three table captions *"External hold-out … not consulted during tuning."* That subsection is **not** the SGSM-overlay ablation (a separate subsection) — so the independence framing exceeds the permitted scope and directly contradicts the main paper. A reviewer reading both documents sees the promoted result reframed from "no independence claim" to "external hold-out validation" depending on which file they open. Fix: reword the supplement's vs-GSK CEC2013 material to "second comparison suite"; confine "hold-out" to the SGSM-overlay ablation subsection per PR-03.

**T-03 (major, prose) — the CEC2017 headline is selection-biased and the exposure is buried.** The tuning disclosure (S5) is admirably candid: CEC2017 was the *development suite*, dimension-tier values were set on CEC2017 `n=51` validation, and *"Six full-panel configurations were compared against the family panel before the final promotion; that count is the multiple-testing exposure of the headline configuration."* But the primary reported result — #1 overall on CEC2017 — is stated in the main text (and conclusions/abstract) with **no co-located caveat** that the configuration was selected on that same suite. On the two suites that were genuinely never touched during tuning, the story is materially weaker: CEC2011 is 2nd with the study's **only Holm-significant unfavorable cell** (loss to eGSK), and CEC2013 is **3rd at D30**. A hostile reviewer's one-line summary is: *on uncontaminated data DT-GSK does not clearly beat eGSK.* Fix: surface the 6-config selection exposure beside the CEC2017 headline (a pointer to S5), so the primary number carries its own caveat.

**T-06 (minor, analysis) — self-init exempts the promoted algorithm from the shared-`X0` fairness rule.** The protocol's fairness rests on all seven optimizers starting from a shared runner-generated `X0`. The single exception is DT-GSK, which draws its own `5·D` initial population and ignores the runner's `X0`. "Same fair-start stream" does not mean the same *points*: a `5·D` self-drawn matrix is not the runner's `X0`, so at low `D` the promoted algorithm and its six comparators do not start from byte-identical populations — a fairness asymmetry that confounds precisely the low-`D` cells where initialization matters most and where the wins are already weak (D10 ties vs agsk/apgsk/fdb-agsk). It is disclosed but never bounded. Fix: either add a control run of DT-GSK started from the shared `X0` to show the low-`D` ranks are robust to the exception, or add it to Limitations as a fairness caveat rather than a mere protocol footnote.

Also feeding this score (covered under T-03): the `D=50` SGSM-activation breakpoint — the exact boundary that defines where the headline mechanism operates — was, per S5, "chosen from first-principles arguments … and **not sensitivity-searched**." Since the tiers were fit on CEC2017, an unvalidated gate coincident with the dev suite's dimension structure is a residual overfitting surface.

## 4. Failure-case-coverage — 3/5 (tickets required)

The named Limitations paragraph (five limitations + statistical-scope coda) is thorough and honest. But two omissions are central to the paper's own thesis, and one internal contradiction is serious.

**T-01 (major, analysis) — the central primitive is credited for high-D gains while its only direct isolation is null, and the reader of the main paper is not told.** ISM/SGSM is "its central primitive … the memory that gives the proposed algorithm its name." The main text repeatedly frames high-D standing as *"consistent with the intended role of the interaction-structure memory"* (IN-02), and the supplement attributes the D100 vs-GSK wins to *"the overlay's design intent of biasing the linkage and local-search operators … on rotated, non-separable landscapes."* **Yet the only experiment that directly isolates SGSM — the 4-cell CEC2013 D50 ablation — is null and points the wrong way:** *full vs. bare-pub = p=0.056, 5 wins / 10 ties / 13 losses.* The supplement honestly concedes the ablation *"cannot cleanly establish"* that SGSM beats bare-pub, and even relabels the 4-cell design's value as a mere *configuration check* rather than a contribution test. So the paper simultaneously (a) credits the overlay for the D100 head-to-head wins and (b) concedes it cannot show the overlay beats the *same algorithm with the overlay off* — the honest reading is that the *scaffold*, not SGSM, may carry most of the high-D result. The main-paper reader is given only the favorable "consistent with" framing and is never told the direct isolation is inconclusive. Fix: add a one-clause main-text pointer at the IN-02 sites that the direct component isolation is reported in the supplement and is inconclusive on the hold-out; align the supplement's design-intent attributions to its own null. (No numbers change; this is a claim-framing/change-control item, hence `analysis`.)

**T-04 (major, missing_evidence) — no structure-learning baseline, despite structure-blindness being the entire motivation.** The introduction and Related Work build the case on GSK's inability to learn structure and position ISM against differential grouping, CMA-ES, and eigen-crossover. The empirical panel is then seven GSK-family algorithms, none of which learn structure. The implicit claim — that cheap accepted-move structure learning is a worthwhile alternative to real structure learning — is therefore never tested. The conclusions list non-GSK baselines as "future work," but a Q1 reviewer will say the natural anchor (at least a CMA-ES reference point at D50/D100, cheap to add) belongs in the paper before the structure narrative can stand. Fix: state explicitly in Limitations that no structure-learning baseline is evaluated (not merely future work), and consider adding one reference optimizer at the two tiers where ISM is claimed to matter.

**T-05 (minor→major, missing_evidence) — NLPSR (a named C3 sub-contribution) is unsupported and mildly harmful at 3 of 4 tiers.** The scaffold ablation's best cell is `ace_de_kr__lpsr__link` (**LPSR**, not NLPSR), and the text concedes *"LPSR beats NLPSR on the D∈{10,30,50} pool; the NLPSR advantage appears only at D=100 and is therefore not captured by this … ablation."* So a named contribution is empirically dominated on the three tiers actually ablated, and its sole justification (D100) was not run. Fix: scope the NLPSR benefit claim to D100 where NLPSR is introduced, or run the D100 scaffold-ablation cell that would substantiate it.

**T-08 (minor, prose) — determinism forces single-thread execution at D≥50, not stated as a practical limitation.** Byte-stable determinism at D≥50 relies on single-threaded numba/BLAS (thread pinning) so the SGSM linear algebra reduces in a fixed order — capping parallel speedup exactly where DT-GSK is most expensive (76 s/run at D100). This deployment-relevant constraint is in the code docs but absent from the paper's Limitations. Fix: one clause in Limitations.

Additional failure cases a reviewer will raise (worth acknowledging even if not ticketed separately): the low-`D` cost/benefit (≈12× GSK compute at D10 to *tie* the cheaper variants) is disclosed as "most expensive" but never framed as a value limitation; and the D30 deficit (both CEC2017 and CEC2013) is attributed to "SGSM inactive below D50" but not otherwise mechanistically explained, given eGSK (whose only structure device is a late SQP) still wins there.

---

## Summary judgment

The evidence discipline, the "no extra *objective* evaluations" scoping, the honest `O(D^2)/O(D^3)` compute accounting, and the negative-findings register are exemplary — overhead-honesty is a genuine 5 and the SGSM-vs-DG distinction is a solid 4. The problems are that (1) the paper's *named central mechanism* is causally unsupported by its own hold-out isolation while the main text carries only the favorable "consistent with" framing; (2) CEC2013's status flips between "no independence claim" (main) and "external hold-out validation, not consulted during tuning" (supplement), breaking the frozen PR-03 rule; (3) the CEC2017 headline is selected on CEC2017 with the exposure buried; and (4) the structure-blindness thesis is never tested against a structure-learning baseline. These are framing/attribution and missing-experiment issues — fixable without touching frozen numbers or the algorithm — but they are central enough to warrant **major revision** before a Q1 venue.
